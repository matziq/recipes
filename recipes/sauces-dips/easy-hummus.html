Easy Hummus

Creamy, smooth homemade hummus that beats store-bought. The secret is the order of blending: whip the tahini and lemon juice on their own first, add the chickpeas in two batches, then thin with a splash of cold water at the end.

Yield: 6 servings (makes 1 1/2 cups) • Prep: 10 min • Total: 10 min

Ingredients

  • 1 (15-ounce) can chickpeas, drained, or 1 1/2 cups cooked chickpeas (250g)
  • 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice, from 1 large lemon (60ml)
  • 1/4 cup well-stirred tahini (60ml)
  • 1 small garlic clove, minced
  • 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for serving (30ml)
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
  • Salt to taste
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons cold water or aquafaba (45ml)
  • Dash of ground paprika, sumac, or za'atar for serving

Instructions

  1. Whip the tahini and lemon juice: In the bowl of a food processor, combine the tahini and lemon juice and process for 1 minute. Scrape the sides and bottom of the bowl, then process for 30 seconds more. This extra time helps whip or cream the tahini, making the hummus smooth and creamy.
  2. Add olive oil and spices: Add the olive oil, minced garlic, cumin, and 1/2 teaspoon of salt to the whipped tahini and lemon juice. Process for 30 seconds, scrape the sides and bottom of the bowl, then process for another 30 seconds or until well blended.
  3. Add the chickpeas: Add half of the drained chickpeas to the food processor and process for 1 minute. Scrape the sides and bottom of the bowl, then add the remaining chickpeas and process until thick and relatively smooth, 1 to 2 minutes.
  4. Thin with water: The hummus will likely be too thick and still have tiny bits of chickpea. Slowly add 2 to 3 tablespoons of cold water or aquafaba with the food processor running until you reach the perfect consistency.
  5. To finish: Taste and adjust as needed with more salt or lemon. Serve with a drizzle of olive oil and a sprinkle of paprika, sumac, or za'atar over the top.

Notes

  • Storing: Keep hummus in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to one week. Freeze it, covered with a thin layer of olive oil, for up to one month.
  • Peeling the chickpeas is optional. It takes about 10 minutes for a can and yields a slightly smoother texture, but the difference is subtle.
  • Aquafaba for fluffy hummus: the thick, starchy liquid from the can of chickpeas can replace the water in the last step; as it whips it creates a lighter, airier texture. Taste it first, and use less salt if it is already salty.
  • Hummus without tahini: add more olive oil in its place, or substitute natural, unsweetened creamy peanut butter.
  • For garlicky hummus, add another clove or use roasted garlic.
  • If the tahini and lemon juice will not cream, there is too little liquid for the blade to catch. Add the olive oil, and a tablespoon of cold water if needed.
  • A food processor works best, but a high-powered blender will do; scrape the sides down a few extra times.
