Yeast-Raised Doughnuts (Susan Reid)

Yield: About 16 doughnuts, depending on cutter size

Ingredients

  • 3 cups (360 grams) unbleached all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup (50 grams) granulated sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon table salt
  • 2 teaspoons instant yeast or active dry yeast
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 cup (227 grams) milk
  • 2 tablespoons (28 grams) butter, melted
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 6 cups peanut oil or vegetable shortening for frying
  • Sugar, glaze, frosting, or filling as desired, optional

Instructions

  1. Stir flour, sugar, salt, yeast, and nutmeg together. Separately mix egg, milk, melted butter, and vanilla, then combine with the dry ingredients to form a soft dough. Cover and rest 5 minutes.
  2. Knead 6-8 minutes until smooth. Put in a greased bowl, turn to coat, cover, and let rise 1 1/2-2 hours until doubled.
  3. Deflate and roll on a lightly floured surface to 1/4-inch thickness. Cut with a 2 1/2-3-inch round cutter, cover loosely, and proof 30-60 minutes until doubled.
  4. Heat the frying fat to 350 F in a heavy pan. Fry 2-3 doughnuts at a time, turning when golden; the source expects no more than about 1 minute per side for this formula.
  5. Lift out and drain on absorbent paper. Verify a sample has no raw center, then add an optional coating or filling.

Optional baked version from the same publisher: after shaping, proof 45-60 minutes, then bake at 350 F for about 14 minutes. Cut into a sample and continue baking if its center is raw.

These quantities and 1/4-inch shaping are for this separate recipe, not the incomplete Berliner formula. Do not transfer this timing automatically to the richer original dough.

Recipe source: Susan Reid, King Arthur Baking, Yeast-Raised Doughnuts. Reviewed November 7, 2026.

About this dish / Did you know?

During proofing, yeast fermentation releases carbon dioxide that helps dough rise. The bubbles explain why rising time is part of many bread methods, rather than merely a pause between mixing and baking.
